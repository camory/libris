# T039 — The ouvrage added from the card

**Backlog line:** Frontend: the ouvrage added from the card. The reader the
app knows already carries their default bookshelf, and the `BookshelfApi` port
with its `FetchBookshelfApi` client already posts the `NewBook` the card holds
and reads back the copy or a failure; the task builds what stands between the
card and that port (D05, D07). Under the card, found or already there, the
full-width primary button *Ajouter à ma bibliothèque*; while the add runs it
reads *Ajout en cours…* with a spinner and accepts nothing; once added, the
copies row shows the new copy on the reader's default bookshelf and the button
is gone, with no message. A new lookup replaces the card, its rows and the
button. Every word from the `fr` catalogue (U04, U05, U06, U08). Component and
view tests over the found, adding and added states. Realises S2 on the
frontend; un-skips `S2 The ouvrage is added`.

**Serves:** spec `bookshelf` S2 and the *Screen* states *Found*, *Already
there*, *Adding*, *Added*; PRD §4.1, §4.3; decisions D04, D05, D06, D07, D10;
screen rules U03, U04, U05, U06, U08. The S2 scenario already expects the
absence row before the add (`6b2235c`, #132), so the T041 contradiction is gone.

**First of its kind:** the **first write the frontend sends to Libris**: the
add's `POST` is the app's first non-GET request, and with it the first request
header the contract does not state, `X-Requested-With`. Also the first screen
with a **second primary button**, which the spec asks for (see *Risks*). The
reviewer repeats this line in its verdict.

## Goal
When this task is done, the reader taps *Ajouter à ma bibliothèque* under a
card and the card itself confirms: its rows name the reader's default
bookshelf, and the button is gone. The add also reaches the real backend,
which refuses a write that lacks `X-Requested-With`.

## Risks and decisions
**The reader is asked on the tap.** The add calls `meApi.currentReader()` and
then posts to its `defaultBookshelf.id`: one more `GET /api/v1/me` per add,
answered network-first by the service worker, and no state in which the card
shows before the reader is known.
- Rejected: reading the reader when the view mounts, as `HomeView` does — a
  tap before `/me` answers has no bookshelf, a state no spec describes.
- Rejected: a Pinia session store — the first store names a convention for
  Tophe, and `HomeView` would move with it.

**The add lives in the view, beside the lookup.** `IsbnView` already holds the
lookup's states and calls its port; the add is the same shape. A first
composable in `application/` would set a naming and a test layout nobody has
stated yet.

**`FetchBookshelfApi` gains `X-Requested-With`.** The client of #132 omits it,
and the backend's chain denies a non-GET without it: every add would fail
after deploy while the mock, which does not know the header, answers `201`.
D07 gives the adapter to the run, and T039 is the first task whose screen
sends the add. The case stubs `fetch` because the contract cannot say it.
- Rejected: a task of its own — S2 would ship broken in between.

**The button is primary, though U04 says one per screen.** The spec's
*Screen* and the line say *full width, primary*, under the card beside
*Chercher*; the brief follows them. The PR body proposes U04's amendment for
Tophe: one primary button per block it serves.

**8 between the card and its button.** U04 puts a primary button 8 under
what it serves and U03 keeps 8 between a control and what belongs to it.
- Rejected: 20 — the button would read as a block of its own.

**`FakeBookshelfApi` records.** A view test proves where the add goes by what
the fake was asked, as `IsbnView.spec.ts` reads `FakeIsbnApi.asked`; the
scenarios keep asserting what the reader sees.

**A late answer.** *Chercher* stays usable while an add runs, and an add
answering after a newer card would join that card's rows. The spec does not
say; `agent/PROPOSED.md`, not this task.
