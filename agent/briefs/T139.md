## Goal
The trick counts only taps in a row, each less than half a second after
the one before; a slower tap starts a new row of its own, so three slow
taps turn nothing on and sound nothing. Turning the beep off (T140) and
keeping it on the phone (T141) build on this pace.

## Risks and decisions
**The tap's instant is the click's `timeStamp`, given to `tap(at)`.** It
is when the reader tapped, carried by the event itself, so the composable
reads no global and its spec writes the instants it needs. Rejected: the
composable reading `Date.now()` or `performance.now()` (a global read
outside `main.ts`, and the handler's time, not the tap's); a timer
closing the row half a second after each tap (a global, a pending timer
to clear at unmount, a spec on fake timers); a clock port (a port for a
number the event already carries).

**Half a second is strict: 500 ms starts a new row.** The spec says
"each less than half a second after the one before" and S2 "more than
half a second": both bounds read the same with 500 outside the row, the
guards of steps 3 and 4 holding it from each side.

**A slow tap starts the new row, it is not dropped**, as the backlog line
says: after it, two quick taps work the trick. Rejected: a slow tap
emptying the row and counting for nothing (a fourth tap then needed,
which no document says).

**No case for the view.** The `h1`'s argument is wiring, proven by S2
over the real `IsbnView`; a view case would prove again what S2 shows.
