**Serves:** spec `fast-entry` — S1 (the BnF's half of it); PRD §3 (edition,
series and tome, publisher, publication year, page count); decisions D02, D04,
D07, D08, D10, D11
**First of its kind:** the first UNIMARC rule that picks between two fields
carrying the same tag, so the first time the record parser reads an
**indicator** and not only a tag and a subfield code — `UnimarcField` grows one
attribute and the mapping learns to ask for it. A smaller first rides along:
the first hand-written **XML** record body in a test, the shape T013 and T024
already used for JSON, written inline to make a case red that no recording can.

## Goal
When this task is done the BnF source maps the records the catalogue has been
writing since about 2019 as completely as it maps the One Piece one: the
publisher out of field 214, the publication year out of the date of
publication, a page count written without its full stop, and the series and
tome out of the title statement when the record carries no 461. It matters now
because the phase's *Done* asks Tophe to scan a manga **and a BD** on the
Pixel and read both cards: today a BD published in the last six years comes
back with no publisher, no year, and — on a provisional record — no pages and
no series, which is most of the card empty for a book the catalogue holds
whole.

## Risks and decisions
- **Where the year sits in field 100.** `100 $a` is a fixed-length text whose
  positions 9 to 12, counted from zero, hold the date of publication; the eight
  before it are the date the record was entered and the ninth a letter. One
  Piece reads `20130718d2013    m  y0frey50      ba`, so the year the existing
  case asserts, 2013, is the same before and after the change — which is why
  the year moves to field 100 without `ONE_PIECE_EDITION` moving. Read the four
  characters and refuse them when the text is too short or they are not four
  digits; the year is an integer, never a `0`.
- **The partial recording loses its field 100.** With the year read from 100,
  `9782723488525-without-pages-and-year.xml` would suddenly name a year and
  `a record without pages and year gives neither` would be false — as would the
  file's name. That file is not a raw recording: its 215 and its `210 $d` were
  already blanked so that Open Library could fill them in `S5 Merged answer`.
  Deleting its `100` datafield is the same act for the same reason, it keeps
  the case and the file name true, and it leaves `S5 Merged answer` green:
  that scenario asserts `publicationYear` 2013, which Open Library's edition
  document gives. The alternative — keeping the field and renaming the case —
  was refused: it would drop the only proof that a record naming no year
  answers `null`.
- **The two-214 recording does not prove the indicator by itself.**
  9782505083399 writes the publisher's `ind2="0"` field *before* the printer's
  `ind2="3"` one, so an implementation that simply takes the first 214 is green
  on it. The discriminating case is the hand-written body of step 5, with the
  two fields the other way round, and it is the case that must be red first.
  Write it inline in the test, as T013 wrote its malformed-author body and T024
  its search body; do not pass a hand-written body off as a recording.
- **Reading an indicator.** `mxc:datafield` carries `ind1` and `ind2`, both
  written as a single character and a space when empty; only `ind2` is needed
  here. `UnimarcField` gains it and the record answers "the field with this tag
  whose second indicator is this"; when no 214 is so marked, fall through to
  210, whose indicators the One Piece record leaves blank. A `controlfield`
  carries neither indicator and is read as it is today (`agent/GOTCHAS.md`).
- **`p` with or without its stop.** 9782371025219 writes `1 volume 348 p`, the
  others `1 vol. (203 p.)`. Making the stop optional is one character in the
  expression; check that the result reads 348 and not 1 — the expression must
  not treat `1 volume` as a page count — and that the three recorded editions
  still answer 203, 52 and 50.
- **A `$h` that names no tome gives no series at all**, rather than a series
  with a null tome: the backlog line reads the series from the title statement
  only "when 200 has a subfield `h` of the shape `tome <n>`". A record with a
  461 is untouched by this rule, and 461 keeps winning when both are there.
- **Two names arrive without a forename.** 9782505125990's `702 $a Jérémy` and
  9782505083399's `701 $a Théo` carry no `$b`, so the mapped name is the
  subfield `a` alone — which `authorsOf` already does. 9782505083399 lists its
  three authors as `700`, `701`, `701` and the expected order is the order of
  the record: Dufaux, Théo, Delaby.
- **9782371025219 is a provisional record**, its date of publication marked
  `u` and its 105 saying the text is not yet described; the catalogue will
  replace it with a final one. That is exactly why it is recorded rather than
  fetched, and why step 1 asks you to check the recording against the criteria
  before trusting them: the values above were read from the live API on
  2026-09-16.
- **`BnfStubs` gains one method.** `answers(isbn, body)` public beside `knows`,
  the shape `OpenLibraryStubs` already has, for the hand-written body alone.
  The class's stubs match on `withQueryParam("query", containing(isbn))`, so
  the body is served for whatever query the source builds; nothing else in the
  class moves.
- **Traps to expect** (`agent/GOTCHAS.md`): keep the `@BeforeAll` HTTP warm-up
  in `BnfSourceTest` under its long timeout — the first HTTP request and the
  first XML parse of a JVM cost more than a second, and `TIMEOUT` is 200 ms;
  `answersTooLate` must keep delaying a real recorded answer; run
  `./gradlew detekt` before each commit and remember it covers the main sources
  only, test sources being checked inside `check`; `ImportOrdering` is
  lexicographic; `ReturnCount` allows two returns; a caught exception must be
  named `ignored`; detekt has no JDK on its classpath, so do not merge the two
  catches of `lookUp` into one. Any command containing `gradlew … check` counts
  as a gate run: never mention it inside another command, run it plainly and
  last.
