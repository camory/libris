## Goal
The series page's card names who made the series and who published it,
each once, translators left out, so the reader sees from the sofa whose
work their shelves hold. A card left with no row is not drawn, so a
series of editions without tome, author or publisher shows no empty box.

## Rules in play
- D05 — who the authors and the publishers are, and their order, are pure
  functions of `domain/` over the books; `SeriesCard` stays
  presentational, props in, no port, and reads them as it reads `runsOf`.
- D07 — the functions in plain Vitest, no doubles; the card mounted with
  the real i18n, asserting its words, never tags or classes; S3's
  `it.skip` turned `it` and nothing else in the scenario file; guards'
  mutations run, reverted, listed in the PR body.
- D10 — PRD words in names (`author`, `publisher`, `book` for a `Book`);
  a test body as Given / When / Then; a file top-down, the exported
  function first, its helpers below; scope `frontend`.
- D11 — the books come in no promised order: the names, their order and
  the spelling kept do not depend on it; the screen sorts with
  `nameOrder(locale)`, the locale the component's `useI18n().locale`.
- U02 — the two new rows are field rows like the others: `body`, the
  label in `muted`, the value in `text`, right-aligned.
- U03 — with no card, the list is the content's first block, right after
  the header's 12; with the card, 20 under it.
- U06 — the authors in the card's order, by their first role, scénario,
  dessin, couleurs, traduction, then by name, ignoring case and accents;
  the names alone, separated by commas; a row with no value has none.
- U08 — every word from the `fr` catalogue; a proper name keeps its own
  spelling, the one a book gives.
- `CLAUDE.md` — TDD; no rationale nor `D##`/`U##` in code.

## Risks and decisions
**Two functions of `Book.ts`, names in, names out.** `publishersOf` and
`authorsOf` take the books, as `runsOf` does, and answer the names in the
order the card shows them, so the card joins them and never sorts. They
sit beside `booksInOrder` since both read books and the spelling rule
below reads the books in that order. Rejected: `Author[]` with roles (the
row shows the names alone); the functions in `Series.ts` over a
`SeriesWithBooks` (the card already hands `runsOf` the books); the rules
in the card (only a mounted component could test them).

**One name is one author, as the screen's collator reads it.** Two names
are one author, or one publisher, when `nameOrder(locale)` compares them
equal: case and accents ignored, as the task line says, which is wider
than the backend's author row, unique on `LOWER(name)` alone, so *René*
and *Rene* do reach the screen apart. Their roles join, so an author is
left out only when every role of theirs, on every edition, is
`TRANSLATOR`. The publisher is a free text of the edition, so the same
rule makes *Hachette* and *HACHETTE* one, as "both ignore case and
accents" reads. Rejected: matching by exact name (two *Goscinny* on one
row); folding inside `authorsInOrder` (changes the lookup card, out of
scope).

**The spelling shown is the first edition's in the order a shelf reads.**
The spec says each once, not which spelling; the answer has no order
(D11), so "first met" would let one page change its words between two
visits. The first edition by `booksInOrder` gives one spelling whatever
the answer's order, and a proper name keeps a spelling a book gave (U08).
Rejected: the first met in the answer; a spelling rebuilt (case changed),
which no book gave.

**No card with no row, and the list's space then.** `SeriesCard` draws no
element when it has no row, so a series of editions with no tome, no
author and no publisher shows its header then its list, which is then the
content's first block, 12 under the header, not 32 (U03). jsdom lays
nothing out, so no test sees the space: the implementer keeps the 20
between card and list and drops it when the card draws nothing, and
checks both on the running app (`npm run dev:mock` and a series whose
books carry a tome, then none). Rejected: the view deciding whether the
card shows (the card's rule written twice).

**S3 un-skipped last, a guard**, as in T103 to T105: by step 16 it passes.
It reads the authors and the publishers in one string, so a row out of
order or a translator left in reds it.
