## Goal
On the ouvrage's page, removing the reader's last copy of the ouvrage
takes the reader back to the screen the page was opened from, or to the
catalogue when the page was opened from its address, and the page leaves
the history. The lookup card (T137) follows.

## Risks and decisions
**The last copy is read from the ouvrage the page holds.** The page knows
every copy of the reader's (`Book.copies`, on all their bookshelves,
whatever the role); when it holds no copy but the one removed, it leaves
without asking. The spec's *Screen* reads "asks the ouvrage anew after a
removal, or goes back": one or the other. Rejected: asking anew and leaving
on the not-found answer (the page draws *Ouvrage introuvable.* between that
answer and the navigation, for a request whose answer the page holds).

**Where the page came from is the history's.** vue-router writes the
previous entry in its history state (`router.options.history.state.back`):
when set, the page goes back through the history, which leaves the page as
a forward entry the phone's back button never reaches; when `null`, opened
from its address, it replaces its entry with `/catalogue`. Rejected: the
previous route kept in a store or a query parameter (a second record of
what the history keeps); a back through the history whatever (opened from a
link, it leaves Libris).

**The view navigates.** The router is `ui`'s and the decision reads the
view's ouvrage, so the view's `afterRemoval` holds it; `useRemoveCopy`
stays as T135 left it. Rejected: a composable taking the router or the
copies (a port or a ui type inside `application/`, for one condition).

**The spinner turns until the page is left.** As T134's fix-up keeps it
turning while the ouvrage is asked again: the question coming back for a
moment before the screen changes is the blink Tophe refused there. How the
view waits for the history's back is the implementer's.
