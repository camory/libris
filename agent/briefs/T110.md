# T110 — The series, an aggregate of the bibliography

**Backlog line:**
> - [ ] T110 Backend: the series, an aggregate of the bibliography.
>       `Series(id, name)` and `SeriesId` in `bibliography.domain.series`,
>       with `SeriesRepository`, whose `findOrInsert(series)` answers the
>       series stored under that name, matched ignoring case (PRD §3, D12).
>       An edition refers to its series by a `SeriesVolume(seriesId,
>       volumeNumber)`; `SeriesEntry(name, volumeNumber)` stays what a
>       source or the reader says. `HoldEdition` finds or inserts the series
>       of a new edition's name and keeps the series the repository
>       answers; `JdbcEditionRepository` writes and reads only the edition's
>       `series_id`. Each answer that names a series reads the series
>       through `SeriesRepository`: the lookup of an ISBN the house holds,
>       the catalogue's page and the series list (D12). The schema is
>       unchanged. No behaviour changes: every scenario test and the
>       contract verification stay green, untouched.

**Serves:** *Series aggregate* phase of `agent/TASKS.md` (no spec; the
amendment of D12 in #285); PRD §3 (*Series, Author and Tag are matched by
name, whatever the capitalisation*); D02, D07, D10, D11, D12.
**First of its kind:** a repository whose write answers the aggregate the
house stores, `findOrInsert`, the name's conflict resolved inside its one
statement; until now a write answers nothing.

## Goal
The series becomes an aggregate the bibliography stores and reads through
its own repository, and an edition refers to it by id, as D12 now states.
T101 and T102 then hand the series' `id` to the series list and page.

## Rules in play
- D02 — `Series`, `SeriesId`, `SeriesVolume` and `SeriesRepository` live in
  `bibliography.domain.series` and import no framework; `JdbcSeriesRepository`
  in `bibliography.infrastructure.persistence`; the library's reads take the
  port, never the adapter.
- D07 — the domain by plain JUnit, the adapter by `@JdbcSliceTest`, the use
  cases over in-memory fakes in `bibliography.fixture`; the scenario classes
  and their DSL untouched; the contract verification runs as many cases as
  on `main`; each guard's mutation run, reverted, listed in the PR body.
- D10 — a `Series` is `series`, a `SeriesId` is `seriesId`, a
  `SeriesVolume` is `seriesVolume`; the port's variable is
  `seriesRepository` (see *Risks*); SQL keywords in upper case; no
  rationale, no `D##` in code.
- D11 — `HoldEdition` mints `SeriesId.new()` (UUIDv7) for the series it
  builds; no migration.
- D12 — a repository reads and writes its one aggregate and joins no
  other: `JdbcEditionRepository` loses its series statement and its join;
  a use case keeps the series `findOrInsert` answers; the series refuses a
  blank name where it is built; it holds no list of its editions; no
  renaming.
- PRD §3 — a series named again in another capitalisation is the one the
  house holds, spelt as it was first stored.
- `CLAUDE.md` — TDD; one step, one cycle, one commit; nothing outside the
  task.

## Risks and decisions
**`SeriesVolume` sits in `bibliography.domain.series`**, beside the
aggregate whose id it carries, as the backlog line places `SeriesId`.
Rejected: `domain.edition` — the edition holds it but does not own the id
it refers to.

**The edition's field is `seriesVolume`**, D10's rule naming a field by its
type. Rejected: keeping `series` — D10 makes `series` a `Series`.

**The port's variable is `seriesRepository`.** D10 names an aggregate's
variable by its name, and *series* is its own plural, so `series` would
name a `Series`, a list and the port at once. Rejected: `series` — it
collides with the `Series` each use case handles.

**`SeriesRepository` has `findById` and `findByIds`**: the held ISBN reads
one series, the catalogue's page and the series list read many, as
`EditionRepository` answers `findByIsbn` and `findByIds`. Rejected:
`findByIds` alone — the lookup would wrap one id in a list; rejected:
`findById` alone — a statement per edition of a page.

**`HeldEdition` carries the `Series`**, read by `BrowseCatalogue`, so the
web reads its name without a repository. Rejected: a `SeriesEntry` built
in the use case — a read model for one field the controller already maps.

**`Series` refuses a blank name** in its `init`, as `SeriesEntry` and
`Edition` do: an aggregate checks its rules where it is built (D12).
Rejected: no rule — the table's `name` would take a blank.

**`SeriesInMemory` matches a name with `lowercase()`**, the fake mirroring
`LOWER(name)` of the unique index, so step 8 runs in plain JUnit; the slice
of step 3 proves the real one. Rejected: an exact match — the fake would
double what the house does not.

**The switch is one cycle, its reads proven by guards.** Changing
`Edition`'s field breaks every reader at compile time, so step 7 cannot be
green with half of them. Rejected: a parallel field during the switch —
two truths about one series; rejected: a split — a first half would leave a
repository nothing calls.

**The capitalisation case's series half moves to `JdbcSeriesRepositoryTest`**:
the edition's write no longer writes a series. The PR body names it.
