## Goal
A word of digits alone in a query is found, beside its prefix match in
`search_text`, in the reader's edition whose tome is that number exactly,
every other word of the query still required. With it S1 runs whole, and
only the near matches (T168) remain of the backend's search.

## Rules in play
- D03 — the tome is read beside `search_text`, from `edition.volume_number`,
  for a word of digits alone, exactly; `search_text`, `search_vector` and
  their index stay as they are; every word required, in any order; the
  catalogue's order, not ranked; a whole ISBN still answered from `isbn13`

  hand-written SQL over `JdbcClient`; no domain type, port, use case,

## Risks and decisions
**Fit.** One run: one clause rewritten, six slice cases, one scenario line.
Rejected: a split (nothing to split).

**The volume number is read beside `search_text`, never written into it.**
D03 says so, and a number in the text would be a prefix like any word, `2`
finding tomes 20 and 21, and would need a re-fill of every stored edition.
Rejected: `tome 2` written into `search_text` (inexact, and a migration).

**A word of digits is told from the lexemes PostgreSQL reads**, the same
words the prefix match asks, through `words_ignoring_case_and_accents`
(T165's decision): its parser reads a run of digits alone as `uint`, so
`2e` (`numword`) and `2.5` are no words of digits. Rejected: splitting the
query in Kotlin (a second tokenizer).

**The tome is the number the digits write**: `02` finds tome 2. *That
number* (S1) is a number, and the word may be written with a leading zero;
no other reading is described. Rejected: comparing texts (`02` finding no
tome). Step 4 pins it.

**A word of digits too long for any tome finds none, never an error.** The
words' route takes every query `Isbn.of` refuses, twelve digits included
(S2, already a case), and any length a reader types; a cast that overflows
answers a 500. Step 5 and the twelve-digit case guard it.

**A query of no word still finds none**, as T165 left it (`& !`, no
lexeme): S1 says only that a blank query answers the catalogue, and a
clause asking each word in turn would turn an empty list of words into
every edition. Step 7 guards it.

**The GIN index may no longer serve the whole clause.** A word asked
either in `search_vector` or in the volume number is an `OR` the planner
may scan for; a household catalogue holds a few thousand editions, and no
test reads a plan. Rejected: an index on `volume_number` (no test
motivates it).
