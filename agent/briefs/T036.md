# T036 — The lookup answers the house's edition

**Backlog line:**
> - [ ] T036 Backend: the lookup answers the house's edition.
>       `library.application`, since the bibliography knows nothing of the
>       library (D02): the lookup takes the reader who asks and looks in the
>       house first — when an edition of that ISBN exists it answers it as the

>       per case of S4.

## Goal
When this task is done a reader asking for an ISBN the house already holds is
answered the house's edition with the copies they can see, each carrying the
name of its bookshelf, without a single call to a source; an ISBN the house
lacks still goes to the sources and is merged exactly as today. Nothing of the
API changes: T037 plugs this use case into the controller and un-skips the
scenarios.

## Rules in play
- D02 — the reader-aware lookup lives in `library.application`: the
  bibliography may never depend on the library, and `ArchitectureTest` fails
  the other way round. `library.application` may depend on
  `bibliography.application` and `bibliography.domain`.
- D02 — one use case is one class with one public method, a `@Service`; the
  ports it takes are interfaces of a `domain`.
- D12 — a rule spanning two aggregates belongs to the use case; aggregates
  refer to each other by id, so `Copy` keeps its `editionId` and the edition
  is fetched by the use case, never held by the copy.
- D12 — a read spanning two aggregates is a query, not an aggregate: its own
  port in the `domain` of the asking context, in the sub-package of its
  concern, answering a read model (data class, no rule, no id of its own, no
  repository) the persistence adapter builds from one query.
- D07 — the scenario class `BookshelfScenarios` is not touched: its six tests
  stay `@Disabled("T037")`. `FastEntryScenarios` stays green untouched.
- D07 — `application` is tested over fakes with plain JUnit and Kotest, `//
  Given` `// When` `// Then`; shared doubles live in a `fixture` package; the
  adapter is proven by a `@JdbcSliceTest` against the given PostgreSQL (D08).
- D10 — no visibility widens for this task: `EditionPreview` gains no public
  constructor, factory or field, `Isbn` gains nothing, `Copy` gains nothing.
  SQL keywords upper case, one statement per `private const val`.
- D11 — ids are value classes; an outcome is a result type the caller reads,
  never an exception; no new table, no migration, no change to `V004__copy.sql`.
- D04 — no contract edit: the pin stays `v0.5.0` in `ApiContractTest`, no
  response body gains a field. The pin moves in T037.

## Test plan
`cd backend && ./gradlew check` is the gate. One step is one case is one
commit; run `./gradlew detekt` before each commit. Commit a step before
checking a guard's mutation: `git checkout <file>` reverts to the last commit.

Persistence first, `JdbcReaderCopiesTest` (`@JdbcSliceTest`, `@Import` of
`JdbcReaderCopies`, `JdbcCopyRepository`, `JdbcEditionRepository`,
`JdbcBookshelfRepository`, `JdbcReaderRepository`, as `JdbcCopyRepositoryTest`
does; `JdbcClient` only if a case reads it):

1. **red** — *a copy on the reader's own bookshelf comes with the name of that
   bookshelf*: one reader, their bookshelf, one edition, one copy. Proves the
   port answers one `CopyOnBookshelf` carrying the copy's id, the bookshelf's
   id and its name. Creates `CopyOnBookshelf`, `ReaderCopies`, `JdbcReaderCopies`.
2. **red** — *a copy on a bookshelf the reader is not a member of is not
   answered*: two readers with their own bookshelf, a copy of one edition on
   each; asking for the first answers only their copy. Proves the query joins
   `membership` on the asking reader.
3. **guard** — *the copies of another edition are not answered*: a second
   edition with a copy on the same bookshelf. Mutation: drop
   `copy.edition_id = :editionId` from the `WHERE`.
4. **guard** — *two copies on one bookshelf are answered one by one*: the card
   counts them. Mutation: `SELECT DISTINCT ON (copy.bookshelf_id)`.
5. **red** — *the copies come ordered by the name of their bookshelf*: two
   bookshelves of the same reader, the copy of the later name inserted first.
   Proves the stated order. If the insertion order happens to satisfy the case
   before the `ORDER BY` exists, treat it as a guard and say so in the PR body,
   with the mutation *drop the `ORDER BY`*.

Then the use case, `LookupIsbnForReaderTest` over fakes:

6. **red** — *an ISBN the house lacks is answered by the sources, with no
   copy*: `EditionsInMemory` empty, the source answering a preview. Proves the
   answer carries the merged preview and an empty list of copies. Creates
   `IsbnLookupResult` and `LookupIsbnForReader`.
7. **guard** — *an ISBN no source knows is unknown*. Mutation: swap the
   `UnknownIsbn` and `SourcesUnavailable` branches of the pass-through.
8. **guard** — *an ISBN whose sources are all down answers that the sources are
   unavailable*. Same mutation.
9. **red** — *an ISBN the house holds is answered as the house holds it,
   without asking the sources*: the house holds an edition for that ISBN, the
   source is armed with a different title. Proves every field of the answer
   comes from the stored edition, and that the source was never asked.
10. **red** — *the copies on the reader's bookshelves come with the edition*:
    the fake query port answers two `CopyOnBookshelf` for that edition and that
    reader. Proves the use case asks the port with the found edition's id and
    the reader who asked, and passes its answer through in order.
11. **guard** — *an edition whose copies the reader cannot see is answered with
    no copy*: the fake holds copies of that edition for another reader only.
    Mutation: make the house branch fall back to the sources when the copies
    come back empty.

## Risks and decisions
**Where the reader-aware lookup lives and what it is called.** It is
`LookupIsbnForReader` in `library.application`, answering `IsbnLookupResult`.
The backlog line and `ArchitectureTest` put it in the library; the name says
what the contract's operation asks (`IsbnLookup`), and PRD §3 keeps *book* for
a stored edition addressed by id, which this answer is not. *Rejected:*
`LookupBookByIsbn` — the answer carries no id and PRD §3 avoids that word here.
*Rejected:* reusing the name `LookupEditionByIsbn` in a second package — two
classes of one name in one code base is a trap for every later import.

**It calls the bibliography use case, it does not redo it.** The house-miss
path delegates to `LookupEditionByIsbn` and maps its three results one to one,
so sources, merge and answers stay unchanged, and `ArchitectureTest` allows it
(`application` may see `..application..`). *Rejected:* a port in
`library.domain` with an adapter wrapping the bibliography use case — a class
that carries no rule, for a dependency the architecture already allows.
*Rejected:* reading `ExternalEditionLookup` directly — it would duplicate the
merge and the fast-entry spec's answers.

**The answer carries an `EditionPreview`, built by the use case.** The contract
has a single `IsbnLookup` schema, with no edition id, whose fields are exactly
`EditionPreview`'s plus `copies`; so both paths answer one `Found` and T037's
controller keeps one mapping. The house's `Edition` is turned into an
`EditionPreview` inside `LookupIsbnForReader`, with the ISBN that was asked.
*Rejected:* two `Found` variants, one per path — two thirteen-field mappings in
the web layer. *Rejected:* a library-side read model repeating the thirteen
fields — the same data a third time. *Rejected:* a public factory on
`EditionPreview` — D10, and the T035 review refused exactly that for
`Isbn.ofThirteen`. If Tophe would rather the conversion sat in the bibliography,
that is a review remark, not a blocker: one private function moves.

**The query port's place and shape.** `ReaderCopies` sits in
`library.domain.lookup`, the sub-package of its concern, as D12 names it, and
answers a flat `CopyOnBookshelf`. *Rejected:* `library.domain.copy` beside the
aggregate — that sub-package holds one aggregate and its repository (D02).
*Rejected:* a method on `CopyRepository` — a repository reads and writes its own
aggregate and joins no other (D12). *Rejected:* a read model nesting a
bookshelf-shaped value — the JSON's shape is the web adapter's, in T037.

**The order of the copies.** `ORDER BY bookshelf.name, copy.id`: a read must
answer in an order the SQL states, and the card lists the copies bookshelf by
bookshelf, so the rows must not move between two identical requests. *Rejected:*
no order at all — the frontend of T038 would show a different order each time.

**Membership, not ownership.** Every member of a bookshelf sees its copies,
whatever their role: S4 says *the bookshelves the reader belongs to*, and the
owner-only rule of T035 is about adding, not reading. *Rejected:* filtering on
`OWNER` — it would hide a shared bookshelf's copies, which S4 never asks for.

**A consequence for T037, not for this task.** The lookup's controller lives in
`bibliography.infrastructure.web` today, and that slice may never see the
library (D02): T037 will have to move the operation's controller to the library
side, or give it a shape that keeps the dependency legal. Nothing here changes
`IsbnController`; the implementer states the consequence in the PR body.

**The sources must be provably unasked.** `LookupAnswering` answers but does not
record, so the house cases cannot prove the silence the backlog line demands.
It gains a list of the ISBNs it was asked. *Rejected:* a second fake beside it —
two doubles of one port. *Rejected:* proving it by the answer alone — the
scenario's claim is that nothing was called, and `BookshelfScenarios` asserts
that with WireMock for the same reason.
