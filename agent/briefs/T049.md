# T049 — The next page

**Backlog line:** Frontend: the next page — when the last row comes into
view and the page received names a next, two skeleton rows stand under it
while the next page is asked with that `after`, and its rows take their
place; under the last row of the last page, nothing. When the next page does
not come, the rows stay and the sentence of S4 sits where the skeleton rows
stood. Realises S2 and the next-page case of S4 on the frontend and un-skips
their tests (full text in `agent/TASKS.md`).

**Serves:** spec `catalogue` S2, S4 (the next page) and the *Screen* states
*Loading more* and *Unavailable* (next page); PRD §4.1; D05, D07, D10, D11;
U01, U02, U03, U05, U07, U08.

**First of its kind:** the first **paged list** on the frontend, and the
first **browser observer in a view**: `CatalogueView` builds the global
`IntersectionObserver` itself, as the scenario that stubs it requires, with
no port between them.

## Goal
When this task is done, scrolling `/catalogue` to its last row asks the page
after it and lists its rows under the first, until a page names no next; a
next page that does not come leaves the rows and says to try again later
under them. *S2 The catalogue comes in pages* and *S4 Libris unavailable, on
the next page* pass; the catalogue spec is then complete on the frontend.

## Rules in play
- **D05**: the pages are the state of `useBrowseCatalogue`, in
  `application/`; `CatalogueView` watches its last row and renders the
  state. No new port; `CatalogueApi`, `FakeCatalogueApi`, `FetchCatalogueApi`,
  `CatalogueRow` and `CatalogueRowSkeleton` unchanged.
- **D07**: gate `cd frontend && npm test`. The composable runs over
  `FakeCatalogueApi` with no mount; the view mounts with the real i18n and a
  provided port, awaits `flushPromises`, asserts text, roles and components.
  Only the two scenario tests above lose their `.skip`, nothing else in
  `CatalogueScenarios.spec.ts` changes.
- **D10**: the composable keeps its one function, `browse`: the verb that
  asks the first page on its first call and the page after the rows listed
  on each later call. Scope `frontend`; trailer
  `Co-Authored-By: Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>`.
- **D11**: the page after is asked with the `next` of the page received, as
  `after`; a page naming no next is the last, and nothing is asked after it.
- **U03**: the message under the rows is a block of the content, 20 under
  the list block, as `specs/catalogue/6-unavailable-next-page.jpg` draws it.
- **U05**: two `CatalogueRowSkeleton` stand in the list block under the last
  row while the next page comes, and leave when it lands; on failure the
  message replaces them. The skeleton rows are no list items.
- **U07, U08, U01, U02**: the message is the one of T048, `IconAlert` and
  `catalogue.error`, `text-body text-danger`; no new word in `i18n.ts`.

## Risks and decisions
**`browse` is the one verb of both pages.** D10 gives a composable one
function; a second call continues where the rows end, and a fresh
composable, built on each arrival, starts at the first page.
- Rejected: a second function `browseNext`/`more`, against D10.
- Rejected: `browse(after)` from the view, which would put the `next` of
  the answer in the view.

**The state names the spec's five screen states.** `loadingMore` carries
the rows listed, and `unavailable` carries the rows it keeps, `[]` on the
first page; the view draws the list block whenever there are rows, the two
skeleton rows in it for `loadingMore`, the message under it for
`unavailable`. The `next` stays inside the composable: the view need not
know it, since `browse` asks nothing after the last page.
- Rejected: `next` or a flag on `listed`, which the view would only pass
  back.
- Rejected: a sixth status for the failed next page, a word the spec lacks.

**No new ask after a failure.** The spec's *Unavailable* state keeps the
sentence under the rows; a new ask when the last row comes back into view
would replace it with skeleton rows, a behaviour the spec does not
describe. The next arrival on the page asks anew.

**The view builds `IntersectionObserver` from the global, no root, no
margin.** The scenario stubs the global and provides no port, so the
mechanism is fixed there; the viewport is the root, and the browser clips
it by `App.vue`'s scrolling wrapper, so "comes into view" is the reader's.
The observer must watch the last row in the render that shows the rows:
the scenario triggers right after `findByText` of the last title, and a
watch later than Vue's post flush fails it on *No row is observed*.
- Rejected: an `ElementVisibility` port with a fake, against the scenario
  as written and a convention no document states.
- Rejected: a sentinel element under the list, when the spec says the last
  row.

**The observer stub lives in the view spec**, beside the scenario's own:
two copies in specs do not yet make a fixture, and `src/fixture` holds
fakes of ports.

**The skeleton rows sit in the list block, outside the `ul`**, so the rows
stay the only list items and the scenario's `rows()` reads the rows alone;
the message sits under the block, 20 apart, as the mockup draws it.
