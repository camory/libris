# T165 — Words find an added edition

**Backlog line:**
> - [ ] T165 Backend: words find an added edition.
>       Each word of a query, ignoring case and accents, is found when it
>       begins a word of the edition's title, subtitle, series' name or one
>       of its authors' names; every word is required, in any order (D03).
>       An edition added keeps what it is searched by with it, and the
>       editions stored before are filled by the migration that brings it,
>       with only the extension it uses: the trigrams' come with T168 (D02,
>       D03). The answer is the catalogue's rows of the reader's editions,
>       in its order and its pages of fifty, `after` naming the last one
>       received (D11), `near` false. Realises S5, an edition added, and S4;
>       un-skips their backend tests.

**Serves:** spec `search` S5 (an edition added), S4, and the words of S1;
PRD §4.2; decisions D02, D03, D07, D10, D11, D12
**First of its kind:** the first PostgreSQL extension (`unaccent`), the
first text search configuration, the first stored generated column
(`edition.search_vector`) and the first GIN index: a migration of a shape
the schema has none of.

## Goal
An edition added is found by the words of its title, subtitle, series'
name and authors' names, ignoring case and accents, each a prefix, all
required; the editions stored before are filled by the same migration. It
gives T164's search its words, on which T166, T167 and T168 build.

## Rules in play
- D03 — `edition.search_text` (title, subtitle, series' name, authors'
  names) written by the adapter on insert; `search_vector` generated from it
  through one configuration, `unaccent` then a copy of `simple`, GIN-indexed;
  each word a prefix, all required, catalogue's order; a whole ISBN still
  answered from `isbn13` alone; `unaccent` alone, no `pg_trgm` (T168), no
  tome (T167).
- D02 — one Flyway SQL migration, `JdbcClient` and hand-written SQL; the
  search's clause in `JdbcCatalogueEditions`, the write in
  `JdbcEditionRepository`; no domain type, port or use case changes: the
  domain never sees `search_text`.
- D07 — the two `@Disabled("search")` lines of S5 (an edition added) and S4
  removed, nothing else in `SearchScenarios`; the words are tested in the
  JDBC slice, where they are computed; no web or controller test; Given /
  When / Then on every case of two statements or more.
- D10 — SQL keywords and functions in upper case; no `!!`; no `D##` or
  rationale in code or SQL; fixtures below the class; no member widened.
- D11 — `V016__edition_search.sql`, three digits; no migration of `main`
  edited; the column added, filled, then made `NOT NULL`, as `V011` does;
  pages of fifty by keyset, `next` the last edition of a full page.
- D12 — `BrowseCatalogue` keeps taking a `CatalogueQuery`, never the text;
  nothing reads a `Series` aggregate to write `search_text` (see *Risks*).

## Risks and decisions
**Fit.** One run: one migration, one statement written, one clause read,
one slice test file, two scenario lines. Rejected: a migration task alone
(a column no test reads).

**The query's words are read by PostgreSQL, through the configuration of
`search_vector`.** The adapter hands the query's `text` to the statement,
which reads its words with that configuration, so a query and a title split
alike on an apostrophe, a hyphen or an accent (probed: `la serpe d'or`
reads `la`, `serpe`, `d`, `or`, as the title does). `to_tsquery` reads its
argument as its own syntax: `&`, `|`, `!`, `:` and parentheses are
operators, and `serpe:* & &:*` is a syntax error (probed), hence step 11.
A query of signs alone reads no word; `@@` of an empty query is false, so
it answers no edition, with no case of its own. Rejected: the words split
in Kotlin, on `CatalogueQuery` (a second tokenizer, diverging from the
configuration).

**`search_text` is written in `INSERT_EDITION`, from the aggregate and the
series' name by its id.** D03 puts the series' name in `search_text`, and
the edition holds only its `SeriesId`; the insert's statement reads the
name of the row its `series_id` names, builds no `Series` and answers
nothing, so no aggregate is read by the edition's repository (D12). The
authors' names are the contributions', which match the stored rows but for
what the configuration ignores. Rejected: the name passed through the port
(the domain would see the search document); the vector computed at query
time from a join (D03 puts the names in `search_text`); an `UPDATE` after
the contributions (a second statement and a row briefly without text).
Traps: `JdbcEditionRepository` holds ten functions and detekt's
`TooManyFunctions` reports eleven; a `NULL` parameter in a variadic
function such as `CONCAT_WS` may need a `CAST`, as the keyset's
`CAST(:after AS uuid)`.

**`V016`, one file.** The extension, the configuration, the columns and
the index come together, `search_text` filled before `NOT NULL` as `V011`
fills. The extension lands in `public` (no schema named), which Flyway's
clean reads; the configuration, depending on `unaccent`'s dictionary, dies
with it (see the gotcha). Editing `V016` between steps is safe until the
merge (`CleanSchema`). Rejected: `IF NOT EXISTS` (nothing else creates
it).

**Names.** `words_ignoring_case_and_accents`, for what it does, as the
collation `ignoring_case_and_accents`; `edition_search_vector`, table then
column, as `series_name_unique`.
Rejected: `french` or `simple` alone (stemming, or accents kept).

**Twelve digits stay a case.** T164 left it for T165: its body holds
through the words, so only its name changes.

**The test helper.** `edition(...)` in `JdbcCatalogueEditionsTest` holds
five parameters and a sixth trips `LongParameterList`: the subtitle (step
4) and the authors (step 6) reach the inserted edition another way, the
implementer's shape.
