## Goal
When Libris does not answer the series, the series page says to try again
later, alone, instead of leaving its skeletons up for good or saying the
series cannot be found. It closes the page's last state of the spec.

## Out of scope

  the page asks anew on its next arrival, as today.
- `FetchSeriesApi`, `problemOf` and `FakeSeriesApi`: a proxy's plain-text
  answer already rejects there, and the fake already rejects with the

- The series list's own failure (`series.error`) and every other screen.
- The offline cache of the PWA: what Workbox answers from its cache is
  not this task's.

## Risks and decisions
**Another problem type reads as unavailable.** T103 deferred any problem
but S5's two to this task; D11 asks a fallback for a key the frontend does
not know, and `useFindBookshelf` reads another problem as no answer, the
same sentence. A problem the contract does not declare for this operation
is not a series that cannot be found. Rejected: keep it `notFound` (says a
series the reader owns does not exist); a third message (no spec words).

**The sentence's key is `seriesPage.error`.** Each screen holds its
messages under its own key, `bookshelf.error` beside `bookshelf.notFound`,
`seriesPage.notFound` already; the series list's `series.error` is another
screen's failure that happens to read the same. Rejected: reusing
`series.error` (a change to the list's words would change the page's).
Unlike T107's `series.cover`, which names one picture on two screens.

**The message takes the not-found one's place and layout.** The mockup
draws the alert and the sentence at the top of the content, as *Série
introuvable.*; how the view picks the sentence per state (two branches, or
a computed message as `BookshelfView`) is the implementer's.

**S6 un-skipped last, a guard**, as in T103 to T107: by step 3 it passes.
