# T073 — An owner adds a member

**Backlog line:**
> - [ ] T073 Backend: an owner adds a member.
>       The readers Libris knows are answered, every one, the asker among
>       them, in no promised order. An owner adds to their bookshelf a reader
>       who does not belong to it, as owner or as viewer: the answer is the
>       new member, and the bookshelf is among that reader's bookshelves, its
>       copies in their catalogue and on the card of their ISBN (PRD §3, D12).
>       Realises S5; un-skips the backend test of S5, both cases.

**Serves:** spec `bookshelves` S5 and its *Contract* section (`GET
/api/v1/readers`, `POST /api/v1/bookshelves/{id}/members`); PRD §3
(*Reader*, *Bookshelf*, *Membership*); decisions D02, D07, D10, D11, D12
**First of its kind:** none

## Goal
`ListReaders` and `AddMemberToBookshelf`, constants since T072, become real:
the readers Libris knows are read from the database, and an add stores the
new membership, so the added reader sees the bookshelf and its copies. Two
readers of the household can then fill one bookshelf, which the rest of the
phase builds on.

## Rules in play
- D07 — the `@Disabled` of `S5 An owner adds a member` is removed, and
  nothing else of `BookshelvesScenarios` changes; its two runs, *added as
  owner* and *added as viewer*, pass. The web layer does not change, so no
  web test is written and `ApiContractTest` stays as it is, green.
- D04 — the pin stays at `v0.11.0`; no contract edit.
- D12 — adding a child is building the aggregate again with the new list
  and calling `update`: `Bookshelf.withMember` answers the new bookshelf,
  and the use case never reads or builds `memberships` itself. The use case
  reads the bookshelf and the added reader each through its own repository.
- D11 — `BookshelfRepository.update` writes the whole aggregate, as
  `JdbcEditionRepository.update` does: the name, then the memberships
  deleted and inserted again. No migration: `membership` holds the rows
  already.
- D02 — `ListReaders` and `AddMemberToBookshelf` depend on `domain` ports
  and `TransactionOperations` only; the update's statements run in one
  `TransactionOperations` block.
- D10 — the repositories are held as `readers` and `bookshelves`; `invoke`
  stays the only public member of each use case; SQL in upper case,
  identifiers in snake_case; files read top-down; the Kotlin layout of
  `backend.md` kept by hand.

## Risks and decisions
**Fit.** One run: two repository methods per port, one aggregate method, two
use cases of a few lines, no web change; the catalogue and the card already
read memberships (`JdbcCatalogueEditions`, `LookupIsbnForReader`), so the
copies follow the new row with no code of their own. Rejected: a split
between the readers and the add (S5 needs both to go green).

**`Bookshelf.withMember` answers the new aggregate.** D12 rebuilds the
aggregate with the new list; the method keeps `memberships` inside
`Bookshelf`, and is where T077 refuses a reader already a member.
`AwaitedCover.attempted(now)` is the tree's precedent of an aggregate
answering its changed self. Rejected: `bookshelf.copy(memberships = …)` in
the use case (the use case reading the child list, D12).

**`update` writes the whole aggregate, name included.** The port's verb is
D11's, and `JdbcEditionRepository.update` writes every column and replaces
the children; a port named `update` that silently drops the name would
mislead its next caller. Rejected: an `addMembership` port method (a child
addressed from outside its aggregate, D12); `update` writing the
memberships alone.

**The two missing lookups answer what the contract names for them.** Kotlin
makes `findById` nullable, so the add must answer something when either
lookup finds nothing: `NoSuchBookshelf` for the bookshelf (what the
constant answered, the contract's `404`), `UnknownReader` for the reader
(the contract's `400`, and a membership naming no reader would fail the
foreign key with a `500`). Steps 10 and 11 pin both as guards. T077 keeps
the asker's role and the duplicate member; its unknown-reader scenario may
then be green on un-skip, which its brief marks as a guard. Rejected:
answering `NoSuchBookshelf` for both (a wrong answer T077 would undo).

**No owner check yet.** The backlog gives the asker's role to T077, so
until T077 merges any reader can add a member to a bookshelf whose id they
know. Staging may run it; the PR body says no release should be tagged
between T073 and T077. Rejected: checking ownership here (T077's red, two
of its four cases).

**Transaction.** `update` is several statements (name, delete, inserts):
they run in one `TransactionOperations` block, so a failure never leaves a
bookshelf without its owner row. Whether the two reads sit inside the block
is the implementer's; step 9 proves the write's boundary. No optimistic
locking (D11): two owners adding at once may lose one add.

**detekt traps.** `ReturnCount` allows two returns: two elvis returns and a
final `return` make three, so the add takes a form within two (a `when`, as
`AddBookToBookshelf` does). A `JdbcReaderRepository` row mapping shared by
three finders becomes a private function; `TooManyFunctions` is far.
