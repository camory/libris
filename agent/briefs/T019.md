# T019 — The barcode scan

**Backlog line:** Frontend: the barcode scan. On `/isbn`, when
`BarcodeDetector` announces `ean_13` and the reader allows the camera, the
camera opens and the first EAN-13 starting with 978 or 979 runs the lookup of
T017 (D05). Where `BarcodeDetector` is absent the screen offers the text field
alone, and the camera stops when the screen goes away. Realises S2; un-skips
`S2 Scanned barcode`.

**Serves:** spec `fast-entry` — S2 and the *Screen* section's *Camera open*
state (and its *Ready* state, see *Risks*); PRD §4.7; decisions D04 (no
contract edit in this task), D05 (the layers: a port in `application`, the
browser behind an adapter in `infra`, the view wired through an injection
key), D07 (`npm test` is the gate, per-layer tests, the scenario un-skipped and
never edited), D08 (only the tools the sandbox has), D10 (strict TypeScript,
identifiers in English, UI text in French, Conventional Commits); rules U01,
U02, U03, U04, U05, U07 and U08 of `docs/DESIGN.md`.

**First of its kind:** the first port whose adapter is a device of the browser
rather than `fetch` — so the first `src/infra/camera/`, the first hand-written
types for a browser API the TypeScript DOM library does not declare, and the
first port a view drives with a start and a stop rather than one call. Also the
first icon button of U04, the first one that toggles its icon and its
`aria-label`, the first `<video>` of the application, the first view with
`onMounted` / `onBeforeUnmount` work, and the first screen that acts before the
reader touches it. What this task chooses for the port's shape, for the
adapter's name and folder, and for the camera block's markup is what the next
device — a photo of a shelf, a torch — will copy: the reviewer repeats this
line in its verdict and Tophe settles the conventions on the pull request,
amending `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` or `docs/DESIGN.md` with what the review
decides.

## Goal
When this task is done, a reader who opens `/isbn` on a phone whose browser has
a `BarcodeDetector` sees the camera under the *Chercher* button, points it at
the barcode of an ouvrage and reads the card of T018 without typing anything;
the cross inside the field closes the camera, the barcode icon reopens it, and
a browser without the detector shows the field alone, exactly as today. It
matters now because `S2 Scanned barcode` is the last skipped scenario of
`specs/fast-entry.md` and scanning is the reason the feature exists (PRD §4.7):
the spec's *Done* asks Tophe to scan a manga and a BD on the Pixel, which
nothing in the tree can do yet.

## Rules in play
- **D04** — the contract is untouched: no file under `api/`, no pin bump. The
  scan produces an ISBN-13 and calls the operation T016 already implements.
- **D05** — the light hexagon, and it is what this task is mostly about: no
  `navigator`, no `BarcodeDetector`, no `MediaStream` handling in `ui`. The
  port and its `InjectionKey` live in `src/application`, the browser adapter in
  `src/infra/camera`, the fake in `src/fixture`, the wiring in
  `createLibrisApp` (the ports interface and one `app.provide`) and in
  `bootstrap` (the real adapter); the view injects the key. `main.ts` keeps its
  monopoly on `import.meta.env` and `window` — the adapter reads `navigator`
  and `globalThis`, which is its job as infrastructure, not the shell's.
  `eslint-plugin-boundaries` is the proof and `eslint.config.ts` is **not**
  touched: `src/infra/camera` already matches the element `infra`, which may
  import `domain` and `application`.
- **D07** — `cd frontend && npm test` is the gate. Each layer is tested in
  isolation: the adapter over stubbed browser globals, the view over the fake
  scanner provided through the injection key, the scenario over the real
  application. No test asserts a tag, a class, a colour or a pixel; every body
  is `// Given`, `// When`, `// Then` unless it is a single statement; shared
  test doubles live in `src/fixture`. The one scenario method the backlog line
  names loses its `.skip` and nothing else in
  `src/scenario/FastEntryScenarios.spec.ts` changes — it is Tophe's file, and a
  scenario that cannot pass unedited is a `blocked` report, not an edit.
- **D08** — no tool the sandbox does not have: no browser automation, no
  device farm, no camera. The camera path is proven by stubs here and by
  Tophe's hand check on the Pixel (the spec's *Proof* says exactly that).
- **D10** — TypeScript strict, ESLint and Prettier, identifiers in English, UI
  text in French, Conventional Commits with the scope `frontend`, branch
  `task/T019-barcode-scan`, the trailer on every commit.
- **U01** — the camera block and the icon name colour roles only: `accent` for
  the icon and the brackets, `border` for the block behind the picture (the
  same role U06 puts behind a cover that has not loaded). No slate, teal or red
  shade in a template, no new role.
- **U03** — the camera block is a block of the content: 20 below the button, 20
  of side padding inherited from `<main>`, radius 14. Every control stays at
  least 44 by 44.
- **U04** — the icon button: the icon alone, 44 by 44, radius 10, no border, no
  background, `accent`, inside the field box at the right; always an
  `aria-label` naming the action in French — *Scanner le code-barres* when the
  camera is closed, *Fermer la caméra* when it is open — and the icon changes
  with it, the barcode becoming a cross. The field keeps its own geometry (50
  tall, 1.5 px `border`, radius 12, numeric keyboard) and its text.
- **U05** — one answer at a time, and the camera is not an answer: "the camera
  sheet is a block of the content, under the button, not a layer over the
  page". No overlay, no modal, no toast. A message and the card keep their
  place under it, and a refused camera shows no message the spec does not
  describe (*Risks*).
- **U07** — `IconBarcode` and `IconClose`, each its own component under
  `src/ui/components/icons`, an inline SVG on a 24 grid, stroke 1.8, round caps
  and joins, `currentColor`, no fill, copied in shape from `IconAlert.vue`. No
  icon font, no icon package. The two brackets-and-line decoration of the
  camera block is drawn in the view's markup, `aria-hidden`, not an icon
  component: it is a frame, not a glyph.
- **U08** — the two `aria-label`s live in the `fr` catalogue and no component
  holds a French string; they are labels of a few words, so no full stop.

## Test plan
Green suite: `cd frontend && npm test`, run plainly. While iterating,
`npx vitest run src/ui src/infra/camera` needs no mock and no network;
`npx vitest run src/scenario` starts `contracteer mock` through the global
setup (about four seconds) and needs `contracteer` on the PATH.

Read first `src/ui/views/isbn/IsbnView.vue` and its spec (the `open`, `ask` and
`field` helpers), `src/application/IsbnApi.ts` and `src/fixture/FakeIsbnApi.ts`
(the shape a port and its fake take here), `src/infra/api/FetchIsbnApi.ts` (the
shape an adapter takes here), `src/ui/components/icons/IconAlert.vue` (the exact
icon template), and `src/scenario/FastEntryScenarios.spec.ts` lines 55 to 66
with its `cameraAllowed` and `cameraSees` helpers — those two helpers are the
specification of the adapter, so read them before writing it.

1. **The port and its fake, first.** `src/application/BarcodeScanner.ts`:
   ```ts
   export interface BarcodeScanner {
     isAvailable(): Promise<boolean>;
     read(into: HTMLVideoElement): Promise<string | null>;
     stop(): void;
   }
   export const barcodeScannerKey: InjectionKey<BarcodeScanner> =
     Symbol("BarcodeScanner");
   ```
   `src/fixture/FakeBarcodeScanner.ts` follows `FakeIsbnApi`: constructed with
   what `isAvailable` answers and what `read` answers (a value, `null`, or a
   promise that never settles), recording `readsInto: HTMLVideoElement[]` and
   `stops: number`; its `stop()` resolves a pending `read` with `null`, which
   is the contract the real adapter honours.
2. **The view, red → green, one case per cycle**, in `IsbnView.spec.ts`. The
   `open` helper gains a second parameter, the scanner, defaulting to a fake
   that reports unavailable, and provides it under `barcodeScannerKey`; the
   eleven existing cases keep their bodies and their assertions. Then, each
   body Given / When / Then:
   - *no detector, no icon*: neither icon button is found and the fake was
     never read from;
   - *a detector, the camera opens by itself*: after `flushPromises()` the
     button named *Fermer la caméra* is there and `readsInto` has one entry;
   - *a code read runs the lookup*: `api.asked` is `["9782723488525"]`, the
     field's value is the ISBN, the card's title is shown and the label is back
     to *Scanner le code-barres*;
   - *the cross closes it, the barcode reopens it*: `stops` is 1 and the label
     is back; a click on it reads the fake a second time;
   - *a refused camera says nothing*: the camera is closed, no message of the
     three is found, the label is *Scanner le code-barres*;
   - *unmounting stops the camera*: `wrapper.unmount()` and `stops` is 1.
3. **The words and the icons.** `i18n.ts` gains `isbn.scan` (*Scanner le
   code-barres*) and `isbn.closeCamera` (*Fermer la caméra*), added as the
   cases above ask for them; `IconBarcode.vue` and `IconClose.vue` are copies
   of `IconAlert.vue`'s template with their own paths, 22 by 22.
4. **The adapter, red → green**, in
   `src/infra/camera/CameraBarcodeScanner.spec.ts`, with
   `vi.stubGlobal("BarcodeDetector", …)` and a `navigator.mediaDevices` defined
   the way the scenario's `cameraAllowed` defines it (copy those two helpers'
   shape; `vi.unstubAllGlobals()` and `Reflect.deleteProperty` in `afterEach`,
   `agent/GOTCHAS.md`):
   - `isAvailable()` false with no global, false when the formats do not list
     `ean_13`, true when they do;
   - `read()` answers the first value `isbn13Of` accepts and skips the others
     (a detector answering `1234567890128` then `9782723488525`);
   - `read()` answers `null` when `getUserMedia` rejects, and `null` when
     `stop()` is called while it is looking;
   - `stop()` calls `stop()` on every track of the stream it opened.
   The implementation: `getSupportedFormats()` for availability;
   `getUserMedia({ video: { facingMode: "environment" } })` — the back camera,
   which is what a reader points at a book — then `into.srcObject = stream`,
   `await into.play()`, then `detect(into)` at once and again every 100 ms
   until a value passes the rule; a `detect()` that rejects counts as nothing
   read and the loop goes on (a real device rejects before the first frame);
   `stop()` clears the timer, stops the tracks, drops the stream and resolves
   the pending promise with `null`, so nothing outlives an unmount.
5. **The wiring.** `LibrisPorts` gains `barcodeScanner: BarcodeScanner`,
   `createLibrisApp` provides it under its key, `bootstrap` builds
   `new CameraBarcodeScanner()`. No test of its own: `vue-tsc` proves the shape
   and step 6 proves the whole chain.
6. **The scenario, last.** Remove `.skip` from `S2 Scanned barcode` (line 55)
   and change nothing else in the file. It boots the real application on
   `/isbn` with the two browser globals stubbed and no interaction at all, so
   it proves the auto-start, the adapter over the stubs, and the lookup over
   `contracteer mock` in one run. Expect it to pass without new production
   code. If it fails, read *Risks* before touching anything: the likely causes
   are a first `detect()` that waits for a timer, a camera block rendered after
   the `<video>` ref is read, or a promise chain longer than
   `findByText`'s 1000 ms budget.
7. **Tidy on green**, then the full gate, then the pull request.

**Not to be written:** a test that asserts the brackets, the scan line, the
block's size or any class (D07); a test of the `<video>` element's attributes;
a test that drives a real camera or a real `BarcodeDetector` (neither exists in
the sandbox, D08); a `BarcodeDetector` polyfill or a second detection library;
a `CameraSheet.vue` component (*Risks*); a test of `vue`'s lifecycle hooks
themselves; a snapshot test; a Playwright test.

## Risks and decisions
- **The camera starts by itself on `/isbn`, decided here — the decision to
  read first.** `S2 Scanned barcode` stubs `BarcodeDetector` and
  `getUserMedia`, opens `/isbn` and expects the card, with no click anywhere;
  the body is Tophe's and D07 forbids editing it, so the camera cannot wait for
  a tap. The spec's *Screen* section pulls the other way: its *Ready* state is
  "nothing under the button" and mockup `1-ready.jpg` shows the barcode icon,
  not a cross. The commit that added the scenario settles it in its own words —
  "S2 (stubbed `BarcodeDetector` + `getUserMedia`, the camera starts by itself
  on `/isbn`)" (`9cb2c43`, PR #50) — so that is what this task builds: the
  camera opens on arrival when the detector announces `ean_13` and
  `getUserMedia` resolves, and *Ready* is what a browser without a detector, a
  reader who refused the camera, and a reader who pressed the cross all see.
  Say it in the pull request body: if Tophe wants the tap of the mockup
  instead, the scenario body changes and that is a spec conversation, not a
  fix.
- **The port is `BarcodeScanner`, with `isAvailable`, `read` and `stop`,
  decided here.** It is named after what it does for the application, not after
  the browser API behind it (`IsbnApi` is named the same way), and `read`
  answering one `Promise<string | null>` mirrors `lookUp` answering one
  `Promise<IsbnAnswer>`: one call, one value, the view's code one `if`. The
  alternatives were a callback (`start(onRead)`, which needs its own teardown
  rules) and an event emitter (a mechanism the tree has none of). `null` means
  "no code: the reader refused the camera, or it was stopped" — the screen does
  the same thing in both cases, so nothing needs to tell them apart, and
  inventing an error type no document describes would be code no test reaches.
- **`read()` takes the `<video>` element, decided here.** The camera picture is
  a block of the content (U05), so the element belongs to the view; the stream,
  `srcObject`, `play()` and the detection loop belong to the adapter. Handing
  the element to the port keeps every browser call in `infra` at the price of a
  DOM type in the port's signature. The alternative, a port answering a
  `MediaStream` the view assigns and plays, puts device plumbing in `ui`, which
  is what D05 exists to prevent. If Tophe prefers the port to own a canvas of
  its own making, that is a `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` amendment on the review.
- **The adapter, not the view, applies the ISBN rule, decided here.** S2 says
  "the first EAN-13 starting with 978 or 979", and a shelf full of barcodes
  answers EAN-13s that are not ISBNs; since `read()` resolves once, the
  skipping has to happen where the loop is. `isbn13Of` is the rule of the
  domain and the adapter may import it (boundaries: infra → domain), so the
  adapter answers the first reading the rule accepts — check digit included —
  and no scan ever produces *ISBN invalide*.
- **The scanned ISBN goes into the field, then `search()` runs, decided
  here.** The view sets `typed` to the value and calls the very function the
  *Chercher* button calls: one lookup path for both entries (the backlog line's
  "runs the lookup of T017"), the reader sees what was looked up, and the
  messages of S4 and S7 keep their meaning under the field. `isbn13Of` runs a
  second time on a value it already produced, which is free and idempotent.
- **The camera block is markup in the view, not a component, decided here.**
  One screen shows it, it has no props and nothing to emit, and the `<video>`
  ref must reach the port — a component would add an indirection and a test for
  behaviour the view's cases already prove. The second screen that opens a
  camera extracts it, exactly as T017 left the message and T018 the rows.
- **The block's height is a portrait ratio until the tab bar exists, decided
  here.** "Fills the space between the button and the tab bar" needs the bar
  and a full-height column, neither of which is in the tree; `aspect-[3/4]`
  under the button is about that space on a 390-wide phone, uses no size U03
  does not have, and changes to the real thing in the tab bar's task. No test
  asserts it (D07); it is judged by the phone check of the spec's *Done*, and
  one bullet in `agent/PROPOSED.md` names it as that task's business.
- **The `BarcodeDetector` types are hand-written in the adapter's file,
  decided here.** TypeScript's DOM library does not declare it. The two
  interfaces the adapter needs live in `CameraBarcodeScanner.ts` beside the one
  function that reads the global, and are not `declare global`: an ambient
  declaration would tell the whole app program the type exists on every
  browser, which is exactly the lie this task exists to handle (and
  `agent/GOTCHAS.md` warns about what goes into the app program). The global is
  read on every call, never captured in a module constant, so the scenario's
  `vi.stubGlobal` — installed after the module loads and removed by
  `vi.unstubAllGlobals()` — is seen and forgotten at the right times.
- **What jsdom does and does not have, verified in this tree on 2026-09-14, so
  the implementer does not guess.** `navigator.mediaDevices` is undefined: the
  scenario defines it with `Object.defineProperty`, and every other scenario
  deletes it in `afterEach`, so the adapter must reach it inside a `try` and
  answer `null` on anything thrown. `HTMLMediaElement.prototype.play` is
  jsdom's `notImplementedMethod`, which is why the scenario mocks it with
  `mockResolvedValue()` — the adapter may `await into.play()`, and the view's
  cases never reach it because the fake scanner never touches the element.
  `srcObject` is implemented nowhere in jsdom (`grep -rn srcObject
  node_modules/jsdom/lib` answers nothing), so assigning the scenario's plain
  `{ getTracks: () => [{ stop: () => {} }] }` is an ordinary property
  assignment: no IDL conversion, no throw, no defensive cast needed.
- **The 1000 ms budget of the scenario.** `showsTheOnePieceCard` awaits
  `findByText("Romance dawn")` with the default timeout, and the whole chain —
  `isAvailable()`, `getUserMedia`, `play()`, the first `detect()`, the lookup
  over `contracteer mock`, the card — has to fit in it. So the first `detect()`
  runs as soon as `play()` resolves, with no timer before it, and the retry
  interval is short (100 ms) and made of `setTimeout`, not
  `requestAnimationFrame`, whose jsdom behaviour depends on the environment's
  `pretendToBeVisual`.
- **`IsbnView.spec.ts`'s `open` helper changes, decided here.** The view injects
  a second port, so every case must provide one; the second parameter defaults
  to a fake that reports the detector absent, which keeps the eleven existing
  cases telling the story they tell today — the typed path on a browser with no
  camera — and makes the scanning cases say explicitly that they are the other
  browser. The helper is extended, never duplicated.
- **The icon button is a sibling of the `<input>`, decided here.** Four
  scenario methods and eleven view cases find the field with
  `getByRole("textbox", { name: "ISBN" })`, whose name comes from the
  `<label for="isbn">`; a button inside that label would put its content in the
  name and make every one of them fail on a query that finds nothing. So the
  field and the button sit in a `relative` wrapper, the button positioned at
  the right inside the box, and the input gains the right padding that keeps a
  thirteen-digit ISBN clear of it.
- **No new dependency.** `BarcodeDetector` is the whole implementation the spec
  names, and a scanning library (ZXing, QuaggaJS) would be a new dependency
  category on a screen that works without it — and the browsers of the house
  (Chrome and Brave on Android) have the API. Where it is absent the field
  stands alone, which is the spec's own answer.
- **The hand check needs a secure context.** `getUserMedia` exists only on
  HTTPS or on `localhost`; the installed app is served over HTTPS, so the
  spec's *Done* works from the Pixel, and `npm run dev` on `localhost` works on
  the desktop. Nothing to do, but say it in the pull request so Tophe does not
  test over a bare IP address and blame the code.
- **The sandbox runs the gate, this box does not.** `npm test` needs
  `contracteer` on the PATH and Node 24, which the sandbox has (D08); on
  Tophe's machine the frontend gate runs inside the image
  (`agent/GOTCHAS.md`). No duration or coverage figure is quoted here: the run
  reports what it measured.
