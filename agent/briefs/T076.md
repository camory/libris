# T076 — A member opens a bookshelf

**Backlog line:**
> - [ ] T076 Backend: a member opens a bookshelf.
>       Opening a bookshelf answers its members alone its name and its
>       members, each a reader's id and display name with their role, the
>       owners first, then the viewers, each by display name ignoring case and
>       accents; a reader who does not belong to it is answered, as for an id
>       naming no bookshelf, that there is no such bookshelf. A read across
>       the bookshelf and its readers, each through its own repository (D12).
>       Unit test of the order.
>       Realises S4; un-skips the backend tests of S4, both cases.

**Serves:** spec `bookshelves` S4 and its *Contract* section (`GET
/api/v1/bookshelves/{id}`, key `404_NOT_A_MEMBER`); PRD §3 (*Bookshelf*,
*Membership*, *Reader*); decisions D02, D04, D07, D10, D11, D12
**First of its kind:** the first file at the root of `library.domain`,
`IgnoringCaseAndAccents`, a comparison of texts shared by two sub-packages'
users (moved out of `BookshelfName.kt`); the first finder over a list of
ids in the `library` context, `ReaderRepository.findByIds`.

## Goal
`FindBookshelf`, a constant since T072, becomes real: a member of a
bookshelf reads its name and its members in the order of S4, anyone else
reads that there is no such bookshelf. T082's screen of a bookshelf, and
T077's refusals, which read the members back, build on it.

## Rules in play
- D07 — the `@Disabled` of `S4 A member opens a bookshelf` and of `S4 A
  member opens a bookshelf, a reader who does not belong to it` are removed,
  nothing else of `BookshelvesScenarios` changes. The web layer does not
  change: `ApiContractTest` already stubs `FindBookshelf` (`Found`, and
  `NoSuchBookshelf` for `404_NOT_A_MEMBER`) and stays as it is, green, its
  `tests=` count that of `main`; no test of a controller.
- D04 — the pin stays at `v0.11.0`; no contract edit.
- D12 — the read spans `Bookshelf` and `Reader`: the use case reads the
  bookshelf through `BookshelfRepository`, its members' readers through
  `ReaderRepository`, applies the aggregates' rules (`hasMember`, `roleOf`)
  and answers `FindBookshelfResult.Found`, shaped for the answer. No
  repository joins the other's table; no query port. The order of the
  roles is a field of `MembershipRole`, `order`, as `ContributionRole.order`,
  never its declaration order.
- D11 — a reader who does not belong and an id naming no bookshelf are one
  outcome, `NoSuchBookshelf`, which the controller already answers `404`
  `/problems/not-found`; no exception crosses a layer. No migration.
- D10 — `invoke` stays the only public member of `FindBookshelf`, its
  signature unchanged; the repositories are held as `bookshelves` and
  `readers`; no visibility widens: the private folding of `BookshelfName.kt`
  moves to a type of its own rather than becoming visible; SQL in upper
  case; files read top-down; the Kotlin layout of `backend.md` by hand.
- D02 — `IgnoringCaseAndAccents` sits in `library.domain`, the JDK alone;
  `FindBookshelf` depends on `domain` only.

## Risks and decisions
**Fit.** One run: a use case of a few lines over two repositories, one
finder, one enum field, one moved function, two un-skips; some twelve
files, no web change. Rejected: a split (the order and the refusal are one
use case, and S4's first case needs the order to go green).

**The readers through `findByIds`, one statement.** D12 has a use case
that needs two aggregates read each through its own repository;
`EditionRepository.findByIds` is the tree's form for many of one aggregate,
empty list included (the gotcha on `IN ()`). A bookshelf always has an
owner, so the use case never passes none, but the port is not to break for
the next caller who does. Rejected: `findById` per member (a statement per
member); `findAll` filtered (every reader read to show a few); a query port
joining `membership` and `reader` (the repositories serve it in two
queries, D12).

**The order composed in the use case, from the aggregates' rules.** The
order spans the bookshelf (the role) and the readers (the display name),
so no aggregate holds it, as S1's order in `ListReaderBookshelves`. The
role's rank is `MembershipRole.order` (D12's rule on an enumeration whose
order means something). Rejected: a `Members` collection type held by
`Bookshelf` (it would carry the readers' names, another aggregate's data,
D12); `compareBy { it.role != OWNER }` (the order of the enumeration said
outside it).

**One folding, in `library.domain`.** S4's names compare as S1's and S3's
do, so the folding T075 placed privately in `BookshelfName.kt` is now
needed by a reader's display name. D10 forbids widening it; a display name
is not a bookshelf name. It becomes `IgnoringCaseAndAccents`, a
`Comparator<String>` at the root of `library.domain`, where D02 puts the
domain's rules and where `bibliography.domain` already keeps what several
of its sub-packages share (`Isbn`, `Kind`). `BookshelfName` keeps its
public members, so S1 and S3 are untouched. This is the *First of its
kind*: Tophe settles the place at review. Rejected: a second copy of the
folding for display names (two copies can part); `BookshelfName`'s
comparator over `BookshelfName(displayName)` (a display name is not a
bookshelf name, and could be refused as one); a top-level function (T075
rejected it, a layout no document states; an `object` is a type); a
`DisplayName` value type on `Reader` (some twenty files for no rule of its
own).

**No transaction.** Two reads and no write, as `ListReaderBookshelves`. A
member added between the two reads is answered or not; readers are never
deleted, so every membership's reader is found.
