## Goal
`DELETE /api/v1/copies/{id}` removes the copy for good when the reader owns
its bookshelf, and answers not found otherwise, as T130 left it; the
edition stays, found again by its ISBN without asking the sources. The
catalogue's page, which reads its editions and their copies in two
statements, drops an edition whose copies left in between instead of
failing, now that a copy can leave.

## Risks and decisions
**Fit.** One run: two port methods with their statements, one use case
that gains its rule, one line of `BrowseCatalogue`, three un-skips, no
migration, no contract change. Rejected: splitting the race off, since it
is born of this task's delete.

**`delete(id: CopyId)` on `CopyRepository`.** D11 makes deletes hard and
names the port's methods after their one statement, so the verb is
`delete`, beside `insert`; it takes the id, all its statement reads, as
`findById` does. Rejected: `remove` (the product's word belongs to the use
case, the port speaks SQL); `delete(copy: Copy)`, which passes fields the
statement ignores.

**`findById` then `bookshelves.findById`, no join.** D12: a repository
reads one aggregate, and the bookshelf answers whether the reader owns it.
A copy that is unknown, or whose bookshelf the reader does not own, is
`NoSuchCopy`, as the contract asks for all of them. Rejected: a statement
deleting where the reader owns the bookshelf, which puts the rule in SQL.

**No transaction around the removal.** Its one write is one statement; a
`TransactionOperations` block at read committed would lock nothing the
check read, and `AddBookToBookshelf` checks ownership outside its block
too. A copy another owner removed between the read and the delete deletes
no row and answers `Removed`, which S7 takes as removed anyway. Rejected: a
block that adds a fake of `TransactionOperations` and proves nothing.

**The race, weighed: the page drops the edition, keeps `next`.** Only
`BrowseCatalogue` reads ids and copies in two statements;
`FindCatalogueEdition`, `FindCatalogueSeries`, `ListCatalogueSeries` and
`LookupIsbnForReader` read the copies first and the never-removed editions
after, so a copy leaving changes their answer, never fails it. An edition
dropped shortens the page by one, and the port's `next` still continues
after its place (D11). Rejected: one transaction at repeatable read over
both reads, a first isolation setting for a rare, harmless race; an edition
kept with no copy, which the catalogue never lists.

**The un-skips are red steps of their own, before the use case.** As in
T130, each is red at the not-found answer, so each is seen red apart, and
step 7 turns the three green.
