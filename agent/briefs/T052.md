## Goal
For an ISBN the house lacks, the lookup answers the sources' cover
candidates in the house's order, and `coverUrl` becomes the first of them.
The card of T060 shows them and T053's add names one; nothing is fetched.

## Rules in play
- D02 — the cover port and the candidates are what the lookup answers, so
  they sit in `bibliography.domain.lookup`; the inventaire.io client sits in
  `bibliography.infrastructure.lookup` and answers within
  `LIBRIS_SOURCE_TIMEOUT` or answers nothing; the amendment naming it is
  proposed in the PR body
- D04 — the pin stays `v0.8.2`; no contract edit
- D07 — *S1 The lookup offers the sources' covers* is un-skipped and changed
  in nothing else; `FastEntryScenarios` *S1 Typed ISBN, found* gains the
  BnF candidate in `covers`, declared in the PR body; no other scenario test
  is edited
- D10 — `LookupEditionByIsbn` keeps `operator fun invoke` alone; the port
  keeps a verb of its own, `lookUp`
- D11 — `coverUrl` is serialised `null`, never omitted; `covers` is always
  present
- D12 — `CoverCandidates` is a collection with a rule of its own (the
  house's order), built through `of(...)` only; the order is a field of
  `Source`, never its declaration order

a step is committed before its guard's mutation is checked. A step marked
red that is green on arrival becomes a guard: its commit body names the
mutation that reds it, checked.

## Risks and decisions
**`100x600`, not the contract example's `480x600`.** Measured live, the
picture service covers the box and never enlarges; only a width that never
binds keeps the height at 600 at most, which S6 needs ("a WebP never needs
scaling"). The example is not asserted by the verifier, which checks the
schema. *Rejected:* `480x600`, over 600 tall for any cover taller than 5:4;
`600x600`, 600 *wide*.

**A port of its own for inventaire.io.** `ExternalEditionLookup` answers a
preview that makes a card; inventaire.io makes none, and its silence must
not turn an unknown ISBN into a failure. `ExternalCoverLookup.lookUp`
answers a candidate or `null`; no record, no `invp:P2`, a failure and a
timeout are all `null`, as S1 tells them apart nowhere. *Rejected:*
inventaire.io as an `ExternalEditionLookup` answering a preview with a
cover alone, which would answer `Found` with no title.

**The hash from `claims["invp:P2"][0]`**, the claim `InventaireStubs`
reads; the address is built on `LIBRIS_INVENTAIRE_URL`. *Rejected:* the
entity's `image.url`, a second reading of one value.

**Candidates from what the adapters answer today.** The BnF and Open
Library candidates are each `Known` preview's `coverUrl`, with the
lookup's `source`: the BnF's when its record has an ark, Open Library's
whenever it knows the edition, unchecked as the spec says. *Rejected:*
candidates on `EditionPreview`, which is also the held edition's and is
merged, where candidates are per source.

**The order is `Source.coverOrder`** (inventaire.io 1, Open Library 2, BnF
3), the cascade order T057 reuses. `INVENTAIRE` is declared after the two
others so that the merge, still ordered by declaration, is untouched.
*Rejected:* a list order fixed in the use case, a rule outside its type.

**The source's name is the edge's.** `IsbnController` names `Source`
through an exhaustive `when`, as the contract's words are the web layer's.
*Rejected:* a display name on the framework-free enum.

**`FreshSources` resets the stubs.** The stubs stay for the context's life
across classes, so *S1*'s Open Library and inventaire.io stubs of
9782723488525 would reach `FastEntryScenarios` *S1* and add two candidates
to its whole body, depending on class order. Every scenario stubs its own
Given, so no case leans on another's stubs. *Rejected:* stubbing
inventaire.io in `FastEntryScenarios` *S1*, a scenario edit D07 forbids.
