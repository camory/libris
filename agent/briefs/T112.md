## Goal
`FindCatalogueEdition` finds the edition of that id in the reader's
catalogue: the edition, its series entry, and its copies on the bookshelves
the reader belongs to, or `NoSuchEdition` when none of its copies sits on
one. The controller, the result type, the response and the contract test
that T111 fixed stay as they are, and S1's scenario goes green.

## Rules in play
- D02 — `FindCatalogueEdition` in `library.application.catalogue` reads
  through the ports of both contexts' `domain`; the controller still calls
  the use case alone; `bibliography` gains no dependency on `library`.
- D07 — S1 of `EditionPageScenarios` is un-skipped and nothing else in that
  file changes; the two S2 cases stay green; the behaviour is proven in a
  plain JUnit test of the use case over the in-memory fakes; no web test,
  no contract test change.
- D10 — the use case keeps `operator fun invoke(readerId, editionId)` as its
  one public member and its ports in variables named as its siblings name
  them (`bookshelves`, `copies`, `editions`, `seriesRepository`); no
  visibility widened; the `@Suppress("UnusedParameter")` goes; no rationale
  and no `D##` in code.
- D11 — no such edition stays a result the controller answers, not an
  exception; the copies come in no promised order and no code sorts them.
- D12 — a read across aggregates and contexts is the use case's: each
  aggregate read through its own repository, the answer the `HeldEdition`
  data class, no query port, no aggregate rebuilt from a join; the series
  entry through `SeriesVolume.entryIn` over the series `SeriesRepository`
  answers.

## Risks and decisions
**Fit.** One run: one use case filled over existing ports, one new test
class of four cases, one un-skip. No migration, no contract or web change.

**The existing ports, no `EditionRepository.findById`.** The edition is
read through `editions.findByIds(listOf(editionId))`, its copies through
`copies.findByEditionId`, the reader's bookshelves through
`bookshelves.findByMember` or `findById` with `Bookshelf.hasMember`, as the
siblings do, and the series through `seriesRepository.findById`; which of
the two bookshelf reads is the implementer's. Rejected: a `findById` on
`EditionRepository` (it changes the port, its fake and
`JdbcEditionRepository`, which sits at ten functions of detekt's eleven,
`backend.md`, for what `findByIds` already answers); a query port joining
copy, bookshelf and membership (D12: the repositories serve this read in a
few queries).

**The membership is read before the edition.** No copy of the reader's
means `NoSuchEdition`, whether the edition exists or not, so the use case
need not read an edition the reader may not see; the edition a copy names
exists, its foreign key says so. This is a statement of what the answer
is, not of the order of statements, which is the implementer's.

**`NoSuchEdition` is driven by S2's scenario at step 2, then guarded at
step 5.** T111 left S2's second case green on a constant precisely so this
task's find could not answer Sam's edition to Léa; step 2's green must
keep it green, so the unit case of step 5 arrives green and is a guard.
Rejected: step 5 before step 2 (it would be green on T111's constant, no
red); a step 2 green answering `Found` with no copies, committed with
S2's second case red (a green step that breaks a green test).

**Steps 3 as red-or-guard, step 4 as guard.** Whether step 2's simplest
green keeps the reader's copies or every copy is the implementer's; the
case stays either way, and the PR says which it was, as T102's steps 4
and 5 did.
